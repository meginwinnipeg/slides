On relational, trust-based learning in libraries bell hooks — their concept of "engaged pedagogy" (especially in Teaching to Transgress) directly supports my vulnerability instinct. They argue teachers must be willing to be changed by students, not just change them. James Elmborg — writes about critical information literacy with a focus on libraries as sites of transformation rather than transaction. His 2006 piece "Critical Information Literacy: Implications for Instructional Practice" was useful. Char Booth — focuses on reflective teaching practice in libraries specifically. Her book Reflective Teaching, Effective Learning is very grounded in intentionality. On agency and space in geography education Paulo Freire — foundational. His Pedagogy of the Oppressed underpins almost everything about creating space for student agency. Harriet Hawkins — works on creative geography and participatory methods. Thinks carefully about how space and encounter shape learning and knowledge-making. Stuart Aitken — writes explicitly about children, youth, and agency in geographic contexts, and takes relational approaches seriously. Bridging both worlds Safiya Umoja Noble — her work sits at the intersection of libraries, information, power, and whose knowledge gets centered.

Data Physicalization


Analog data visualizations: encompasses anything that isn’t digital data visualization - data physicalization, data materialization, crafted data experiences "data materialization" refers to the practice of translating abstract, conceptual information into physical 3D objects or installations- then mention some folks just call this 'art'.

Assessment


Intentionality as practice. I'm trying to not just do things- but take the time to think about why I'm doing them. That's a form of reflective practice that has a theoretical home. Agency-centered pedagogy. I try to create space for students to find their own agency- that puts me in conversation with self-determination theory, student-centered learning, and possibly participatory or emancipatory frameworks. Relational authenticity. On the vulnerability point- research backs me up. Instructor self-disclosure, done purposefully, lowers power distance and increases psychological safety. Students ask more questions, take more intellectual risks, and trust the learning environment more. You're not being unprofessional; you're being human. Impact over output/ . I measure the value of my work by the change it creates in learners, not by publication metrics. That's a genuinely different orientation than most of academia rewards- I don't know what to do about that
